Low customer retention

Users may lose interest after the initial excitement of LEVELUP and stop using the platform, reducing recurring subscription revenue. This could occur if challenges become repetitive or users feel they are not making sufficient progress.

Cash flow forecast

analysis

The forecast indicates that LEVELUP experiences negative cash flow during its first two months as the business establishes its customer base and incurs relatively high fixed operating costs. From September onwards, increasing subscription revenue allows the business to generate positive monthly cash flow, with the closing cash balance reaching approximately $228,209 by June.

Seasonality may influence this forecast. During the Australian summer, extreme heat can make exercise less appealing, potentially reducing engagement. However, LEVELUP's focus on home-based workouts reduces this effect. January may also experience stronger demand as customers establish New Year's fitness goals. Student examination periods could similarly reduce engagement, making cash reserves important during periods of slower customer growth.

02

Break even

analysis

Break-even analysis determines the number of subscriptions LEVELUP must sell before its revenue covers its fixed and variable costs. The monthly subscription price is $24.99, while the estimated variable cost of servicing each subscriber is $3.50. This provides a contribution of $21.49 per subscriber towards fixed costs and profit.

LEVELUP therefore needs approximately 878 paying subscribers per month to reach break-even. The business is projected to exceed this level during its first year, with subscriber numbers reaching approximately 950 by October. This demonstrates that the subscription model can cover operating costs relatively early while providing a foundation for future profitability.

01

Subscription revenue

$0 – $49.99 / month

LEVELUP will use a tiered subscription model to generate recurring revenue while catering to customers with different levels of engagement and spending. The Free plan provides basic workouts, tasks and XP, allowing users to experience the platform before upgrading. The Core plan at $24.99 per month provides the full LEVELUP experience, including AI form tracking, personalised workouts, XP progression, challenges and leaderboards. The Pro plan at $34.99 per month unlocks additional challenges, increased XP rewards, advanced AI feedback and exclusive competitions, while the Ultimate plan at $49.99 per month includes all Pro features as well as the LEVELUP fitness kit and exclusive rewards. This tiered model creates multiple recurring revenue streams, encourages customers to upgrade as their engagement increases and supports long-term customer retention.

Market research

and industry analysis
01Industry context

The global fitness industry continues to experience strong growth, driven by increasing awareness of health and wellbeing, rising demand for digital fitness solutions, and advances in wearable technology. At the same time, the gaming industry has become one of the world's largest entertainment sectors, with millions of young people spending significant amounts of time playing competitive video games. These two expanding industries present a unique opportunity for LEVELUP to combine gaming and fitness into a single platform that addresses declining physical activity among young people.

02Primary research

Primary market research was conducted through surveys and interviews with high school and university students aged 15–25. The results showed that while many participants wanted to become more physically active, the main barriers were lack of motivation, boredom, and difficulty maintaining consistency. Many respondents indicated they would be more likely to exercise if workouts incorporated rewards, progression systems, achievements, and competition similar to the games they already play.

01Lack of motivation 02Boredom 03Difficulty staying consistent
03Secondary research

Secondary research further supports this opportunity, demonstrating that gamification increases user engagement, motivation, and long-term exercise adherence compared with traditional fitness applications. Despite this growing demand, many existing fitness platforms focus primarily on workout tracking or instructional content rather than creating an immersive gaming experience. This gap in the market positions LEVELUP to provide a differentiated solution that transforms exercise into an engaging and competitive activity.

04Market sizing

The market opportunity can be analysed using the TAM–SAM–SOM framework. The Total Addressable Market (TAM) includes all individuals worldwide who participate in fitness activities or use digital fitness platforms. The Serviceable Available Market (SAM) narrows this to young people aged 15–25 who regularly play video games, own smartphones, and are interested in improving their fitness through technology. The Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) focuses on Australian gamers aged 15–25 who are most likely to adopt LEVELUP during its launch phase before the business expands into international markets.

Pricing strategies

These were specifically designed around making the price seem more attractive to customers, rather than simply explaining how the price was calculated.

01

Psychological pricing

LEVELUP will price its core subscription at $24.99 rather than $25.00. Although the difference is only one cent, $24.99 can be perceived as significantly cheaper because consumers tend to process prices from the leftmost digit. This makes the subscription appear to be in the $20 range rather than the $25 range.

LEVELUP can present the different subscription tiers alongside each other to establish a price anchor. Displaying the higher-priced Pro and Ultimate plans makes the $24.99 Core plan appear more affordable and better value, while also encouraging customers who want additional features to upgrade.

01

Identified problem

and introduction

Physical inactivity among teenagers and young adults is becoming an increasingly significant issue in Australia. Many individuals struggle to maintain consistent exercise habits due to a lack of motivation, expensive gym memberships, and fitness experiences that fail to keep users engaged over time. Existing fitness applications often focus solely on tracking workouts rather than creating an enjoyable and rewarding experience.

Market research conducted during the development of LEVELUP identified that many respondents preferred a fitness platform that incorporated competition, rewards, and progression systems similar to those found in video games. This highlights a clear gap in the market for an innovative solution that combines fitness with gamification.

02

Products

and services

LEVELUP is a gamified fitness platform designed to transform exercise into an immersive and rewarding experience. The application utilises artificial intelligence to provide personalised workout recommendations while incorporating experience points, achievement systems, leaderboards, and multiplayer challenges to encourage long-term engagement.

The prototype has been intentionally designed so that every feature directly addresses an identified customer need. For example, the AI fitness coach provides users with tailored workout plans based on their goals and fitness levels, while the XP system rewards consistency and encourages users to progress through increasingly challenging milestones. Additionally, multiplayer challenges foster a sense of community and accountability, allowing users to compete with friends and other members worldwide.

Registration

As an Australian startup, LEVELUP will be required to complete several registration processes before commencing operations. The business name will be registered through the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC), and an Australian Business Number (ABN) and Tax File Number (TFN) will be obtained to ensure compliance with taxation requirements. Structure, ownership

and team

Initially, LEVELUP will operate as a sole trader business. This structure has been selected as it provides complete ownership and control, enabling faster decision making and reducing the administrative burden commonly associated with larger business structures.

However, as the business grows and seeks external investment, there is potential for LEVELUP to transition into a proprietary limited company (Pty Ltd). This would provide limited liability protection and create greater opportunities for expansion, partnerships, and capital investment. By adopting a phased approach to ownership, the business can remain flexible while supporting long-term growth objectives.

During its initial stages, LEVELUP will operate with a relatively small organisational structure consisting of the Founder and Chief Executive Officer, a software developer, a marketing manager, a customer support officer, and a graphic designer. This lean structure enables the business to remain cost-effective while ensuring that all critical functions are adequately managed.

As the business expands, additional personnel, including project managers, data analysts, and sales representatives, may be employed to support increasing demand and facilitate future growth.

06

Legal

considerations

As LEVELUP collects personal and health-related information, compliance with Australian privacy legislation is essential. The business will adhere to the Privacy Act 1988 and implement appropriate safeguards to ensure the protection of customer data. It must also comply with Australian Consumer Law, which governs consumer rights, product guarantees, and advertising practices.

To further protect the business, LEVELUP will obtain public liability, professional indemnity, and cyber security insurance. These policies will provide financial protection in the event of legal disputes, cyber incidents, or other unforeseen circumstances.

Camera footage is sensitive information. Form tracking films users while they train, so that footage is treated as sensitive health-related information under the Privacy Act 1988.
